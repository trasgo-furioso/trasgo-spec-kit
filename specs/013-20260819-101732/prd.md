# PRD: Trasgo Spec Kit — Spec-Driven AI Development Bundle

**Created**: 2026-08-19
**Discovery Session**: 2026-08-19
**Status**: Opportunity

## Problem Statement

**Pain Point**: AI-assisted product development requires a spec-driven mindset, but teams are stuck in vibe coding or layering engineering harnesses on top of a fundamentally broken process. Three specific breakdowns occur:

1. **Vibe coding trap** — developers use AI without specs, get inconsistent results, and conclude AI doesn't help. Some report being more efficient without AI.
2. **Fragmented documentation** — product managers generate docs with AI but they drift from implementation and still require manual information transfer up and down the chain.
3. **Wrong work rhythm** — developers sit in front of Claude Code micromanaging it instead of delegating properly specced tasks and moving on to other work.

The core paradigm shift these teams are missing: programming now happens in English, not Python. Instead of writing code, teams write markdown files that describe logic and algorithms — and that's hard to see at first.

**Who**: Small-to-mid software agency teams (1-10 people) that build apps for clients. Roles include UX designers, frontend/backend devs, QA, product managers, and engineering managers. They are technically skilled software developers but AI is new tech that requires a change in approach and paradigm — that's the strongest challenge.

**Current Alternatives**: Teams use Claude Code, Cursor, or similar tools inconsistently. They consume courses (often marketed via Instagram) that teach harness patterns, progressive disclosure, CLAUDE.md optimization, and other engineering techniques. These courses miss the point: they treat AI as a better code generator and add more engineering layers on top of a process that's broken before engineering. Nobody is packaging the full paradigm shift — from discovery to implementation — as an opinionable, enforceable process.

**Desired Outcome**: Teams deliver features in days, not weeks by following a structured spec-driven workflow:

1. **Discovery** — team dumps what they know, identifies assumptions and user journeys, fills gaps with stakeholders, produces an accurate PRD.
2. **Specify** — use cases are extracted from the PRD.
3. **Plan** — implementation approach is decided, contracts defined.
4. **Tasks** — work is broken into phases.
5. **Implement** — AI handles the implementation heavy lifting from well-defined specs.

The process creates natural parallelization points: once the plan defines contracts, work fans out (e.g., backend/frontend in parallel). Spec artifacts are the coordination mechanism, not meetings or tickets. Discovery involves stakeholders; specs and planning are collaborative within the team; tasking and implementation can be done individually. Works for multi-disciplinary small teams or a single person with product sense and engineering knowledge.

## Jobs to Be Done

- When I receive a client feature request, I want to structure it into specs my team can execute on, so we deliver in days and increase margin.
- When I leave a planning meeting with a vague task, I want to turn it into a well-defined spec, so AI can do the implementation heavy lifting while I move on to the next thing.
- When I'm exploring a product idea, I want to articulate my vision into structured artifacts, so I can validate assumptions before committing engineering effort.

## Assumptions

- Teams want to ship faster — that's the core expectation from AI adoption.
- The bottleneck is process, not tooling — better harnesses and prompting techniques won't fix a workflow that skips specs.
- Small teams (1-10) can adopt an opinionated process — they're agile enough to change how they work, unlike large orgs with entrenched processes.
- Spec-writing is a learnable skill — devs and PMs can learn to "program in English" even though it feels unnatural at first.
- The process is transferable — what works for one person after 2 years can be packaged and replicated by other teams via a bundle.
